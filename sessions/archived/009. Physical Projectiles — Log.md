# Session 009 — Physical Projectiles — Log

**Date:** 2026-09-12
**Branch:** session-009/1
**Roadmap section:** Pass 9 — Physical Projectiles

## What was built

### Phase 1 — `Config`

- Added per-weapon projectile properties: `BallistaProjectileSize/Mass/Friction/Restitution` and
  `CatapultProjectileSize/Mass/Friction/Restitution` — a light, fast ballista ball and a heavy,
  bouncier catapult ball (`CatapultProjectileRestitution = 0.45`, above the block's own `0.1`).
- Dropped `BallistaImpact`/`CatapultImpact` (manual impulse-scale constants — knockback is now real
  collision physics), `BallistaGravityScale` (no more faked gravity; real bodies share one real
  gravity), `MovingBlockKillSpeed` (no more velocity-threshold contact check), and
  `ProjectileLifetime` (a real body settles on its own; round reset clears it either way).
- Added `PrincessMass`, `PrincessFriction`, `PrincessRestitution` (feed a `PhysicalProperties` the
  same way `BlockMass` does for blocks) and `PrincessTopplingAngle` (degrees from upright before
  she counts as knocked over; started at `60`).

### Phase 2 — `WeaponManager` — real bodies instead of a raycast probe

- Replaced the anchored, `CanCollide=false` hit-probe with a real ball per weapon: unanchored,
  `SetNetworkOwner(nil)` immediately after unanchoring (mirrors `BlockManager.releaseAll()`'s
  ordering), one initial `AssemblyLinearVelocity` set from `launchVelocity`, no more manual
  per-frame integration or raycast stepping.
- `onHit`/`hitParams` (the manual impulse-and-despawn raycast logic) removed outright — a real
  physical body just collides. The direct-hit report to `PrincessMonitor` went with it; a
  projectile now only ever kills her by physically toppling her, via `PrincessMonitor`'s own tilt
  check.
- `clearAll()` now tracks every projectile spawned this round (a plain list), not just ones still
  "active" mid-flight, so a landed, at-rest ball still gets cleared at the next round's reset.

### Phase 3 — Princess becomes a real physical object

- `Arena.build()` gives the Princess a `PhysicalProperties` derived from `PrincessMass` and her own
  volume, same pattern as block density.
- `Arena.resetRound()` now re-anchors her (ATTACK can leave her unanchored and tipped over) in
  addition to repositioning her upright.
- `PrincessMonitor.arm()` unanchors her and calls `SetNetworkOwner(nil)`, mirroring
  `BlockManager.releaseAll()`'s ordering, at ATTACK start.
- Replaced both prior kill paths (`Touched`-by-fast-`BlockId`, and `WeaponManager`'s raycast-
  reported direct hit) with one `Heartbeat`-driven check: compare her `CFrame`'s up vector to world
  up, and once that angle passes `Config.PrincessTopplingAngle` she's dead, reason `"toppled"` —
  no distinction between what caused it. `Types.RoundResult.reason` narrowed from
  `"survived" | "direct" | "crushed"` to `"survived" | "toppled"`.

### Phase 4 — `AttackInput` — ballista aim preview under real gravity

- Unified the catapult's and ballista's aim-preview trace into one `stepTrace(origin, velocity)`
  that steps an initial velocity forward under real gravity and raycasts each step — the ballista's
  shot really does drop now (no counter-force trick, per the "settled in conversation" decision),
  so its preview needed the same stepped treatment the catapult's arc always used. The old
  single-raycast `straightTrace` is gone.

### Phase 5 — `ResultBanner` — updated reason text

- `REASON_TEXT`'s `direct`/`crushed` entries collapsed into one `toppled` entry ("knocked off her
  feet"), matching `Types.RoundResult.reason`'s new shape.

### Phase 6 — Balance and UX follow-ups (from playtest feedback, beyond the original brief)

This session ran through many playtest-and-adjust rounds after the original 5 phases landed green.
In order:

- **Ground friction.** A real ball rolling without slipping has nothing to stop it under Roblox's
  idealized physics — friction alone doesn't kill pure rolling motion. Added
  `Config.GroundFriction`/`GroundRestitution` and gave the `Ground` part its own
  `CustomPhysicalProperties` so a landed catapult ball settles in a reasonable time.
- **Camera cutting away too early.** `MatchController` used to broadcast `phase = "RESOLVE"` the
  instant the last shot was fired (shot count hitting zero), which made `AttackCamera` snap back to
  third-person before the shot had even landed, and the round-result banner felt like it "popped up
  immediately." Fixed in three parts: `AttackCamera` now stays engaged through the whole RESOLVE
  phase (roles don't swap until the round-end hold finishes, so `state.roles.attacker` is stable
  throughout); `MatchController` now holds at RESOLVE for `Config.RoundEndHoldTime` after
  broadcasting the outcome, instead of swapping roles / starting the next round immediately (a new
  `ContinueRequested` remote lets either player skip the wait early, wired to a "Continue" button
  added to `ResultBanner`, which no longer auto-fades); `ResolveSettleTime` grew `1.5`→`3` seconds
  so the actual win/loss determination isn't made before a slower shot has finished landing.
- **Builder's camera carrying over stale orientation.** Added a new `BuildCamera` client module:
  snaps the camera to a fixed, consistent view (behind-and-above the character, same framing
  `CameraIntro` releases into) the instant the local player transitions into the Builder role at a
  BUILD-phase edge — not on every StateChanged during BUILD, so it never yanks the view mid-build.
  Skips its very first (priming) call so it doesn't fight `CameraIntro`'s own release tween at
  match start.
- **Princess distance and color.** `PrincessDistance` doubled `14`→`28`. This alone would have
  pressed her almost flush against the back boundary wall under the old hardcoded
  `PlatformSize.Z = 30`, so the builder platform's depth was changed to derive from
  `PrincessDistance` (plus a clearance constant) instead of a fixed number. Recolored her pink
  (was grey/stone) per direct request — same plain box shape.
- **Block scale experiment.** To push balance in the opposite direction (the wall-building puzzle
  was judged "interesting" but not yet "matters"), `PieceCount` dropped `9`→`6` and `BlockSize`
  tripled (`1.25`→`3.75` per side, 27x volume) in one change — all downstream geometry (build zone,
  grid pitch/columns/rows, platform sizing) is formula-derived from these two constants, so nothing
  else needed touching. `BlockMass` was deliberately left at `25` (unchanged) — it's a pure balance
  knob decoupled from geometry, matching how this codebase has always treated it, not a real
  material density.
- **Build-zone-width exploit.** Diagnosed and fixed a real exploit: the build zone was narrower
  than the platform (`PLATFORM_MARGIN`-wide side lanes on each side), so a ballista shot could
  bounce off the boundary walls and reach the Princess without ever passing through the wall at
  all — the Builder had no way to block that. Removed `PLATFORM_MARGIN`'s contribution to
  `platformHalfWidth` entirely; the build zone now spans the platform's full width, edge to edge.
- **Block count adjustment.** `PieceCount` raised `6`→`8` (+2) after the width-exploit fix and a
  round of testing on the smaller roster.
- **Princess toppling angle.** Diagnosed the game as still "far too easy" to win via a two-shot
  catapult combo (break the wall, then snipe the now-exposed Princess with a second shot) — the
  wall-building puzzle was praised as interesting, but the finishing blow was too cheap. Raised
  `PrincessTopplingAngle` `60°`→`78°` as the most surgical single-number fix: a clean hit shouldn't
  reliably end the round on its own.

## Playtest result

Many rounds of two-player Studio playtesting across the whole session, not one pass. Confirmed
working, in order: the catapult ball bounces and settles, the ballista reads as distinctly
lighter/faster, both persist after landing; the ground-friction fix stopped balls rolling
indefinitely; the camera/outcome-hold fix let players actually watch the final shot land instead of
being cut away; the Builder's camera reset to a consistent view each round; the wider build zone
closed the bounce-around-the-wall exploit; the larger/fewer-block experiment and the raised
toppling angle both registered as real difficulty changes in the intended direction. User's
running assessment: the physical-projectile mechanic and the block-scale experiment together made
the game "pretty challenging on both sides" — good news for the wall-building puzzle, but the
underlying balance problem (the two-shot catapult combo) was diagnosed precisely enough by the end
of the session to scope four dedicated follow-on sessions around it, rather than keep tuning
single numbers indefinitely.

## Config changes

- Removed: `BallistaGravityScale`, `BallistaImpact`, `CatapultImpact`, `MovingBlockKillSpeed`,
  `ProjectileLifetime`, `PLATFORM_MARGIN` (internal local, not a `Config` field, but worth noting —
  its effect on platform width was removed, not just the constant).
- Added: `PrincessMass = 60`, `PrincessFriction = 0.7`, `PrincessRestitution = 0.1`,
  `PrincessTopplingAngle` (`60` → raised to `78` during the session),
  `BallistaProjectileSize/Mass/Friction/Restitution` (`{1,1,1}` / `6` / `0.3` / `0.2`),
  `CatapultProjectileSize/Mass/Friction/Restitution` (`{1.6,1.6,1.6}` / `35` / `0.5` / `0.45`),
  `GroundFriction = 0.8`, `GroundRestitution = 0.05`, `RoundEndHoldTime = 10`.
- Changed: `BlockSize` `1.25`→`3.75` per side (27x volume); `PieceCount` `9`→`6`→`8`;
  `PrincessDistance` `14`→`28`; `CatapultMinForce` `80`→`70`, `CatapultMaxForce` `150`→`120`;
  `ResolveSettleTime` `1.5`→`3`; `PlatformSize` derivation changed from a fixed `30`-stud depth
  plus a width margin, to a depth derived from `PrincessDistance` and a width with no margin
  (exactly the build zone's own width).

## Notes for next session

Session ended with a design conversation, not just a playtest report. The user asked for research
into the source board game (*Crossbows and Catapults*, Lakeside 1983) for balance/complexity ideas;
findings (via web search — see this session's own transcript for sources) were: warriors as a
defense layer separate from the wall (have to be knocked down before the hero can be hit at all),
one-action-per-turn alternation (not a multi-shot burst), plural win conditions, and
attrition-with-recovery rather than instant loss. The board game has no progression/leveling
system at all (it's a physical toy, no persistence between plays) — the user's separate interest in
weapon leveling/unlocks was flagged as a different design problem to solve later, not something to
find in the source material.

Four features were agreed as the next four sessions, in this order, displacing "Tune, then launch"
to Pass 14 (this close's own roadmap update reflects this):

1. **Pass 10 — Guard piece** (brief already drafted: `sessions/010. Guard Piece — Brief.md`). A
   physical obstacle between the wall and the Princess, directly closing the two-shot-combo
   problem. Fixed/server-placed, not Builder-controlled, for this first pass.
2. **Pass 11 — Builder's timed shield.** A once-per-round ability: a small protective dome over the
   Princess for about half a second, timed by the Builder to an incoming shot. First real Builder
   agency during ATTACK (today it's pure spectation once the wall is confirmed).
3. **Pass 12 — Scoring & points.** Replace binary round win/loss with points: wall pieces remaining
   (not toppled off the platform) plus a Princess-toppled bonus worth roughly a quarter of the
   maximum wall-piece score — intended to make "grind the wall for points" a viable Attacker
   strategy alongside "hunt the Princess." Changes `Types.RoundResult`'s data shape; real open
   design questions (what counts as a surviving "piece" once one piece can split into several
   independent bodies; what "not toppled off the base" precisely means) deferred to that session's
   own Open Gate.
4. **Pass 13 — Weapon spin & trajectory/bounce.** Add spin to firing (harder to aim precisely) and
   rework trajectory/bounce feel — likely real angular velocity plus a per-frame curving force, not
   a cosmetic-only effect. Its own tuning cycle, scoped separately since it's the least proven idea
   of the four.

Only Pass 10's brief exists so far (drafted this close, per the project's normal flow of drafting
the next brief at the end of a session). Passes 11-13 are agreed in shape but not yet drafted —
each should be drafted from the template once the session before it closes, same as always.
