# Session 013 — Weapon Spin & Trajectory/Bounce — Brief

**Roadmap section:** Pass 13 — Weapon Spin & Trajectory/Bounce (`roadmap.md`)
**Branch:** session-013

## Goal

Add real spin to weapon fire and rework trajectory/bounce feel so the arc genuinely curves —
likely real angular velocity at launch plus a per-frame Magnus-style force — rather than a
cosmetic-only spinning mesh. Spin should make aiming meaningfully harder, per the roadmap.

## Starting state

- Session 012 is implemented: points-based scoring replaces binary win/loss. The Builder scores
  per surviving wall piece (body-level, `BlockManager.survivingCount()` vs. an orientation-drift
  threshold, `Config.BlockTopplingAngle = 60`); the Attacker scores a fixed `Config.PrincessKillPoints`
  bonus (15 — tuned to ~1/4 of the theoretical max wall score of 60) if the Princess topples.
  `RoundResult` carries the full points breakdown; `ResultBanner` and `Hud` show points instead of
  rounds won.
- Mid-session tuning from the same playtest, not itself Pass 12 scope: `Config.CatapultProjectileMass`
  raised 35 → 43.75 (+25%, more hit force without changing trajectory — mass doesn't affect
  ballistic curvature under Roblox's gravity), `AttackInput`'s `METER_LEG_TIME` cut 3.4s → 2.4s
  (30% faster launch meter), and `Config.ShotSequence` extended from 3 shots
  (`Catapult, Catapult, Ballista`) to 5 (`Catapult, Catapult, Catapult, Ballista, Ballista`) since
  the wall was still too hard to bring down. Expect to tune spin against these current numbers.
- `WeaponManager.fire()` today: spawns a real unanchored ball, sets one initial
  `AssemblyLinearVelocity`, and hands everything else to Roblox's physics engine — no angular
  velocity, no per-frame force of any kind. `AttackInput`'s predictive trace (`stepTrace`) is a
  matching pure-ballistic step (gravity only), which is why the crosshair and dashed line
  currently match the real flight path exactly.
- Rojo pipeline: known-good (`rojo build` after every change, no lint/type tooling installed).

## Plan

### Phase 1 — `Config`

Add spin tunables once the open questions below are settled: angular velocity range (per weapon,
or a single shared range — depends on which weapon(s) get spin), and a Magnus-style curve
coefficient governing how strongly spin bends the arc.

### Phase 2 — Server: `WeaponManager`

Apply an initial `AssemblyAngularVelocity` at launch. Add the curving force — likely a
`VectorForce` (or a per-frame `Heartbeat`-applied force) proportional to spin × velocity,
scaled by the new coefficient, active only while the ball is airborne.

### Phase 3 — Client: trajectory preview

Decide how (or whether) `AttackInput`'s predictive trace accounts for the new curve — matching
the server's curving math keeps the crosshair trustworthy but duplicates that logic client-side;
a simplified or no-curve preview is cheaper to build but means the crosshair visibly lies once
spin is in play.

### Phase 4 — Playtest

Two-player Studio playtest: confirm spin is visible and the arc actually curves (not just a
spinning mesh), that the ballista's flat-trajectory identity and the catapult's arcing/imprecise
identity both still read distinctly per spec, and that the preview built in Phase 3 doesn't feel
broken or misleading.

## Open questions

Settle these at the Open Gate — the roadmap leaves all three unresolved:

- **Which weapon(s) get spin.** The roadmap says "one or both weapons." Catapult-only preserves
  the ballista's spec-identity as a precise flat shot; adding it to both makes the ballista
  harder to use in a way the spec doesn't call for. This decides whether Phase 1's Config shape
  is one shared spin range or two independent ones.
- **Randomized or aimed spin.** The roadmap's own framing ("adds spin to firing (harder to aim
  accurately)") reads as randomized — spin as an accuracy penalty, not a controllable skill
  mechanic — but should be confirmed before Phase 1, since an aimed/controlled version would need
  its own input and HUD readout.
- **Client trace fidelity.** Whether `AttackInput`'s predictive trace gets the same curving math
  as the server (accurate preview, more client code duplicating server physics) or stays a
  straight-gravity approximation (cheaper, but the crosshair will visibly mismatch the real
  flight path once spin is active).
