# Session 010 — Guard Piece — Brief

**Roadmap section:** Pass 10 — Guard Piece (`roadmap.md`)
**Branch:** session-010

## Goal

Add one physical obstacle between the wall and the Princess, so a broken wall doesn't leave her a
free, unobstructed target. This directly answers session 009's closing finding: a catapult shot
that clears or knocks down the wall, followed by a second catapult shot arcing straight onto an
exposed Princess, wins the round with no recourse for the Builder. The guard piece is a second
layer of defense, separate from the wall itself — inspired by the source board game's warriors,
which have to be knocked down before the hero behind them can be hit at all.

## Starting state

- Session 009 is implemented and playtested: projectiles are real, persistent, unanchored physics
  bodies (a heavy bouncy catapult ball, a light fast ballista ball); the Princess is a real
  unanchored body whose death is a continuous tilt-angle check (`Config.PrincessTopplingAngle`,
  currently `78`) against world up, armed at ATTACK start (`PrincessMonitor.arm()`, mirroring
  `BlockManager.releaseAll()`'s unanchor-then-`SetNetworkOwner(nil)` ordering) and disarmed at
  RESOLVE.
- Balance tuning already applied this session (all in `Config`, all still first-pass and
  expected to move again): `PrincessDistance = 28`; the build zone/platform now share the same
  width with no side margin (closes a bounce-around-the-wall exploit); `PieceCount = 8` and
  `BlockSize` tripled to `3.75` per side (an active experiment in the opposite balance direction
  from the original 12-block roster); `GroundFriction`/`GroundRestitution` added so a landed ball
  settles instead of rolling indefinitely.
- The still-open problem this session solves: with the wall down (by design or by a good hit),
  nothing stands between the attacker's second shot and the Princess. There is currently no
  concept of a second obstacle, a shielded zone, or a Builder-placed defender independent of the
  12-piece wall queue.
- The Attacker's camera now stays engaged through RESOLVE (session 009), and the round holds on
  the outcome for `Config.RoundEndHoldTime` before advancing — unrelated to this session, but
  worth knowing the camera won't cut away before a guard-piece hit (or miss) is visible.
- Rojo pipeline: known-good (`rojo build` after every change, no lint/type tooling installed).

## Plan

### Phase 1 — `Config`

Add tunables for the guard piece: size, mass/friction/restitution (a `PhysicalProperties`, the
same pattern `BlockManager`/`Arena` already use for blocks and the Princess), and its position
relative to the Princess or the build zone. Exact values are first-pass guesses, same as
everything else in `Config` — the point of this phase is picking reasonable starting numbers, not
final tuning.

### Phase 2 — `Arena` — spawn, position, and reset the guard piece

Spawn one guard piece per round, positioned between the build zone and the Princess (close enough
to her that a shot landing on or near the Princess is likely to meet the guard first — exact
placement is a judgment call to make during implementation, not specified numerically here).
Decide during this phase, based on what reads better in Studio: a plain box like the Princess's
own placeholder shape, or something that visually reads as a "guard." `resetRound()` needs to put
it back and re-anchor it each round, the same way it already does for the Princess.

### Phase 3 — Make it a real physical obstacle

At ATTACK start, the guard needs to become a real, unanchored, server-owned body (unanchor then
`SetNetworkOwner(nil)`, same ordering as blocks and the Princess) so it can actually be knocked
down, knocked aside, or destroyed by a hit — a real obstacle, not a scripted invulnerable wall.
Confirm during this phase whether "destroyed" needs an explicit rule (e.g. despawn once knocked
over) or whether simply being physically displaced is enough to let subsequent shots through, the
same way a real wall block already works.

### Phase 4 — Verify emergent behavior, don't hand-roll it

`WeaponManager` and `AttackInput`'s aim-preview trace shouldn't need any code changes — a real
solid body should already participate in ordinary collision and raycasts the same way a block or a
landed projectile does. Confirm this holds in Studio rather than assuming it; if either needs a
change, that's a sign the guard piece isn't behaving like a normal physical object and worth
flagging before proceeding.

### Phase 5 — Playtest

Two-player Studio playtest specifically targeting the problem this session exists to solve: after
the wall comes down (or is aimed around), does the guard piece give the Builder real protection —
does a second catapult shot that would have been a free kill now have to deal with the guard
first? Confirm the guard itself can be destroyed/displaced with enough effort (this isn't meant to
make the Princess unkillable, just to remove the free, unobstructed second shot).

## Out of scope

- The Builder does not choose the guard's placement or get any control over it this session — it's
  a fixed, server-spawned fixture, not one of the Builder's placed pieces. Giving the Builder
  agency over a defensive piece is closer to Pass 11's timed-shield ability; revisit if this
  session's playtest suggests a fixed guard isn't enough on its own.
- Pass 11 (Builder's timed shield), Pass 12 (scoring/points), and Pass 13 (weapon spin/trajectory)
  — all separate sessions, per the plan agreed at session 009's close.
- Any further tuning of session 009's still-fresh balance changes (`PrincessDistance`, block
  size/count, `PrincessTopplingAngle`) beyond what's needed to make the guard piece itself read
  correctly in a playtest.

## Open questions

Settle these at the Open Gate:

- **Guard durability.** Does the guard piece behave exactly like a wall block (knock it far enough
  and it's out of the way, no special-cased "destroyed" state), or does it need its own explicit
  removal condition? Leaning toward the former (simpler, consistent with "a real physical
  obstacle, not a scripted rule") unless there's a reason to want something more deliberate.
- **Guard appearance.** Plain box (fastest, matches the Princess's own current placeholder look)
  or something that visually reads as a defender? Not load-bearing for the mechanic either way.
