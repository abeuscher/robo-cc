# Session 009 — Physical Projectiles — Brief

**Branch:** session-009

## Goal

This session solves two separate problems with one change, and they shouldn't be conflated
during implementation or playtest:

1. **Make ATTACK more interesting.** Projectiles become real physical balls that persist on the
   board after they land, instead of a raycast probe that despawns on first contact. Both
   weapons throw a ball (not a puck -- see "Settled in conversation" below); the catapult's is
   heavy with a little bounce, the ballista's is light and fast. Both read as real thrown
   objects, not a targeting laser with a model glued to it.
2. **Make ATTACK less effective, for balance.** Reduce both weapons' force/impact so knocking
   down a wall is harder than it is today, and change the Princess's win condition itself from a
   touch/velocity check to requiring her to actually be knocked over. This is a separate knob
   from (1) -- a physically "interesting" projectile that still one-shots every wall doesn't
   solve the balance problem, and a weaker-but-still-abstract raycast probe doesn't solve the
   interest problem. Both need doing, and they can be tuned independently of each other later.

## Reading list

## Starting state

- Session 008 (randomized piece generation) is implemented and playtested green: pieces with
  isolated cells confirmed to drop as separate physical bodies at ATTACK start.
- The current ATTACK-phase projectile model, precisely, before this session touches it:
  - `WeaponManager.makeProjectile` spawns an anchored, `CanCollide = false`, `CanQuery = false`
    ball-shaped `Part` -- explicitly *not* a physics body, by design (its header comment: "a
    deterministic hit probe, not a physics body").
  - `WeaponManager.step` (on `Heartbeat`) manually integrates each projectile's own `velocity`
    table field (gravity faked via a per-weapon `gravityScale`, not a real physics property),
    raycasting from its last position to its new one each frame against a fixed include-list
    (blocks, the Princess, the arena boundary). The ballista's `BallistaGravityScale = 0.05` is
    what fakes its near-flat trajectory -- it is not, and cannot be, a property of a real
    unanchored part.
  - `WeaponManager.onHit` applies a manual `ApplyImpulse` to a struck block (scaled by a
    per-weapon `Impact` constant and the block's mass) and reports a direct hit to
    `PrincessMonitor` if the raycast lands on her -- then the projectile always despawns,
    whether it hit something or not.
  - `WeaponManager.clearAll()` (a round-reset hook) destroys every entry in `active`, a table of
    currently-in-flight projectiles only. There is no persistent per-round list of projectiles
    that have already landed, because none currently exist -- everything despawns on its first
    hit or after `Config.ProjectileLifetime`.
  - None of this survives real, persistent projectiles unchanged: `step`'s manual integration,
    `onHit`'s impulse-and-despawn, and `clearAll`'s active-only cleanup all assumed a projectile
    that either hits something once and vanishes, or expires. A real physics body does neither.
- The Princess, precisely, before this session touches her:
  - `Arena.build()` creates her as an ordinary `Part` through the generic `part()` helper, which
    always sets `Anchored = true`. She has never been a physics body -- she's a static target
    that gets logically flagged dead, but her `CFrame` never changes from anything but
    `Arena.resetRound()`'s own explicit position reset (upright, at a fixed point).
  - `PrincessMonitor` currently has two independent, unrelated kill paths: a raycast landing on
    her directly (reported by `WeaponManager`), and `Touched` by anything carrying a `BlockId`
    attribute above `Config.MovingBlockKillSpeed`. **This session replaces both** with a single
    physical condition -- neither survives, not even generalized; see Phase 3.
  - `Arena.resetRound()` only resets her `CFrame` (position, implicitly upright). It has never
    needed to touch `.Anchored` because she has never been anything but anchored.
- Rojo pipeline: known-good (`rojo build` after every change, no lint/type tooling installed).

## Settled in conversation

These were decided before this brief was finalized, restated here so they don't need
re-litigating at the Open Gate -- only confirming:

1. **Both weapons throw a ball, not a ballista puck.** A flat puck sliding under real physics
   risks tumbling or catching an edge unpredictably (it isn't rotationally symmetric); a ball
   behaves cleanly and predictably no matter how it's struck. Differentiate the two weapons by
   mass and speed instead of shape.
2. **The ballista is light and fast; the catapult is heavy and slower/arcing.** Under Roblox's
   default gravity (no air drag), *all* masses fall at an identical rate regardless of weight --
   mass has no effect on trajectory curvature. What keeps the ballista reading as "flat" is
   speed: fired fast, gravity has little time to act before it crosses the trench, so the arc
   stays visually shallow even though it's technically still a parabola. Making it *light* (not
   "dense") is what serves the balance goal -- less mass means less momentum on impact even at
   high speed, so it hits softer than the catapult's heavy ball without needing to be slower.
   This also settles Phase 2's open question from an earlier draft: no counter-force/anti-gravity
   trick needed, just accept the real (subtle) arc.
3. **The win condition becomes physical: the Princess must be knocked onto her side.** Replacing
   both of today's kill paths (a precise aimed-line check, and a touch+speed threshold) with one
   geometric check -- has her own tilt passed some angle from upright -- rather than merging or
   generalizing the two old mechanisms. This is a bigger swing than a tuning change: she becomes
   a real unanchored physical object for the first time, watched continuously rather than
   triggered by a discrete event, and no longer cares at all *what* hit her or how fast, only
   whether she's still standing. That's also a real difficulty increase on its own -- a graze
   that would score a kill today might not tip her -- independent of and additive with (1)'s
   force reduction.

## Plan

### Phase 1 — `Config`

- Add per-weapon projectile properties: both a ball shape/size; the ballista's lighter and
  faster (`BallistaMuzzleVelocity`/`BallistaMinVelocity` likely *keep* their rough magnitude or
  even increase, since speed is now doing real work, not just a cosmetic number for the launch
  meter); the catapult's heavier, slower, with some bounce (a restitution value above the
  block's `0.1`).
- Drop `BallistaImpact`/`CatapultImpact` (manual-impulse-scale constants) -- knockback is now
  real collision physics, driven by mass and velocity, not an applied impulse.
- Drop `Config.MovingBlockKillSpeed` -- there's no more velocity-threshold contact check at all
  once the win condition is purely orientation-based.
- Add `PrincessMass`/`PrincessFriction`/`PrincessRestitution` (she needs real physical
  properties now that she's an unanchored body) and `PrincessTopplingAngle` (degrees from
  upright before she counts as knocked over -- a genuinely new tunable, not a re-derived one, so
  a reasonable starting value like 60 is fine to pick now and adjust at Pass 10).
- `ProjectileLifetime` likely becomes dead weight (a real physics body settles to rest in finite
  time on its own) -- confirm during Phase 2 whether anything still needs it before removing it.

### Phase 2 — `WeaponManager` — real bodies instead of a raycast probe

- Replace the anchored hit-probe with a real ball per weapon, unanchored, `CanCollide = true`,
  `SetNetworkOwner(nil)` immediately after unanchoring (same ordering discipline blocks already
  use at `releaseAll()`), initial `AssemblyLinearVelocity` set once from `launchVelocity` -- no
  more manual per-frame integration. Roblox's own physics engine takes over gravity, collision,
  and bounce from there.
- This makes "blocked by any standing wall, cannot arc over" (spec, ballista) emergent from real
  collision rather than a raycast include-list -- the ball physically hits the block and stops.
  Verify this still reads correctly once it's no longer a deliberate rule but a side effect of
  physics.
- Knockback on a struck block: start by trusting Roblox's own collision response (real mass and
  velocity transfer) rather than keeping a manual impulse -- simpler, and directly serves the
  balance goal. Only add a supplemental impulse if Phase 6's playtest finds it feels too limp.
- Round-reset cleanup (`clearAll`) needs to track and destroy every projectile spawned this
  round, not just ones still "active" mid-flight -- a landed, at-rest projectile still needs
  clearing at the next round's reset.
- `onHit`'s raycast-triggered direct-hit report to `PrincessMonitor` goes away along with the
  raycast it depended on -- a projectile now kills her (or doesn't) purely by whether it
  physically topples her, exactly like a falling block would, via Phase 3.

### Phase 3 — Princess becomes a real physical object

- `Arena.resetRound()` needs to re-anchor her (not just reposition her) each round, since ATTACK
  can now leave her unanchored and tipped over -- without this, the next round would start with
  a Princess who's still subject to physics, possibly with leftover velocity, teleported into a
  pose that looks upright but isn't stable.
- `PrincessMonitor.arm()` unanchors her and calls `SetNetworkOwner(nil)`, mirroring
  `BlockManager.releaseAll()`'s ordering for blocks -- she becomes a real physical target the
  moment ATTACK begins, not before.
- Replace both existing kill paths (`Touched`+attribute+speed, and the raycast-reported direct
  hit) with one `Heartbeat`-driven check, while armed: compare her `CFrame`'s up vector against
  world up; once the angle between them exceeds `Config.PrincessTopplingAngle`, she's dead,
  reason `"toppled"`. No attribute checks, no `Touched` connection, no distinction between what
  caused it -- a block landing on her, a wall collapsing onto her (including the Builder's own,
  preserving that specific spec intent), or a projectile strike all funnel through the same
  physical outcome.
- `PrincessMonitor.reason()` now only ever returns `"toppled"` (or is irrelevant when she
  survived) -- `Types.RoundResult.reason` narrows from `"survived" | "direct" | "crushed"` to
  `"survived" | "toppled"`.

### Phase 4 — `AttackInput` — ballista aim preview under real gravity

- With no counter-force (per "Settled in conversation"), the ballista's shot now really does
  arc, however subtly, over the trench. `AttackInput.straightTrace`'s aim preview currently
  assumes a literal straight line with no drop at all, which will visibly lie about the impact
  point once the real projectile drops even a little. Give it the same step-and-raycast treatment
  `arcTrace` already does for the catapult, just parameterized for the ballista's own (much
  flatter, faster) arc rather than assuming zero drop.

### Phase 5 — `ResultBanner` — updated reason text

- `REASON_TEXT`'s `direct`/`crushed` entries collapse into a single `toppled` entry (e.g.
  "knocked off her feet") matching `Types.RoundResult.reason`'s new shape.

### Phase 6 — Playtest

Two-player Studio playtest: the catapult ball visibly bounces once or twice before settling, the
ballista ball is visibly faster/lighter and reads distinctly from the catapult's, both persist
and remain visible/solid after landing, a wall takes more hits to bring down than today, and the
Princess only goes down when she's actually knocked onto her side -- confirm a graze or a light
tap that would have killed her before now doesn't, and that a real topple (a block landing on
her, or a solid hit) does.

## Out of scope

- Exact final values for reduced force/mass/restitution/`PrincessTopplingAngle` -- Pass 10,
  "Tune, then launch."
- Any BUILD-phase change (session 008's territory) or match-loop change (session 004's).
- The client's aim-preview crosshair predicting *past* first contact -- it will still show where
  a shot first touches something, not where it ends up after bouncing. That's an accepted
  approximation for this pass, not a defect to fix now.
- A supplemental manual knockback impulse, unless Phase 6's playtest specifically asks for one
  beyond what real collision physics already provides.
- Any visual/animation polish on the topple itself (e.g. a forced fall animation) -- she's a
  plain `Part`, and physics alone deciding how she topples is the point of this pass.
